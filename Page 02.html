Architectural positioning note. ClaraVis operates SAP S/4HANA as the system of record for ERP, procurement, and finance[cite: 4]. SAP IBP handles demand planning[cite: 4]. Ariba manages strategic sourcing across four regional instances[cite: 4]. The Autonomous Supply Chain does not replace any of these[cite: 4]. It delivers intelligence across the enterprise — spanning the supplier network, the manufacturing sites, the quality management system, and the CSRD obligations that no single SAP module boundary contains[cite: 4].
GDPR Data Processing Framework. The Autonomous Supply Chain processes operational data only — no patient personal data is ingested directly[cite: 4]. Supplier data (financial signals, performance records) is processed under Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interest (supply chain risk management)[cite: 4]. Contract and procurement data is processed under Art. 6(1)(b) contractual necessity[cite: 4]. QualityTrace ingests device serial and batch records linked to MDR events — processed under Art. 6(1)(c) legal obligation (MDR Article 87)[cite: 4]. A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is mandated under Art. 35 for all five high-risk AI models prior to go-live[cite: 4]. Data controller: ClaraVis Medical Systems GmbH[cite: 4]. Data processor: Google Cloud Germany GmbH (DPA and SCCs executed)[cite: 4]. All personal and sensitive operational data remains within the europe-west3 VPC-SC perimeter — architecturally enforced, not policy-only[cite: 4].
FDA 21 CFR Part 820 — Operationalised. ClaraVis manufactures MRI and CT imaging systems sold in the US market through its North America entity[cite: 4]. Part 820 (QSR) requires: Design History File (DHF) per device, Device History Record (DHR) per unit, and documented CAPA workflows[cite: 4]. QualityTrace addresses this directly: automated device genealogy traces produce DHR-compliant records queryable from a single API call; CAPA workflows are structured within the NCR resolution pipeline; DHF linkage is maintained via immutable audit store cross-referencing Veeva Vault document IDs[cite: 4]. See BR-09 for the dedicated FDA traceability requirement[cite: 4].

Each stakeholder below has a primary pain point, a specific capability the Autonomous Supply Chain must deliver for them, and a documented adoption risk or concern that must be addressed in the change management plan[cite: 4].

Section 03

AI Readiness Audit

Where ClaraVis Medical Systems stands today — five dimensions[cite: 4]. The AI Readiness Assessment is the input to the TOGAF Architecture Vision[cite: 4]. It defines the starting position and frames the gap the Autonomous Supply Chain is designed to close[cite: 4]. Scored 1–5[cite: 4]. Findings are actionable, not evaluative[cite: 4].

Data Maturity
3 / 5
SAP IBP BigQuery exports, Ariba REST, Veeva Vault REST, Salesforce REST, and 6 freight portals via Pub/Sub[cite: 4]. Data exists but fragmented across systems with no unified event fabric or feature store[cite: 4].
Action: Unified Pub/Sub event fabric + BigQuery feature store as AS Day 1 infrastructure[cite: 4].
ML Maturity
1 / 5
No production ML models[cite: 4]. Excel-based demand planning[cite: 4]. Supplier risk via annual questionnaire[cite: 4]. No Vertex AI Pipelines, no Model Registry, no drift detection[cite: 4]. No XAI capability[cite: 4].
Action: Vertex AI Pipelines + Model Registry + monitoring jobs — mandatory before first model promotion[cite: 4].
Compliance Infrastructure
1 / 5
MDR traceability is manual[cite: 4]. EU AI Act compliance not yet assessed[cite: 4]. CSRD Scope 3 is a manual quarterly process[cite: 4]. Five ML models intended for production have no SHAP or HITL design[cite: 4].
Action: XAI layer + HITL spec + Model Cards + risk documentation — all required at go-live[cite: 4].
Integration Readiness
3 / 5
SAP S/4HANA, Ariba (4 regional instances), IBP, Salesforce, Veeva Vault, and Excel/SharePoint all present[cite: 4]. Integration patterns defined (Pub/Sub, REST, BigQuery export) but not yet unified[cite: 4].
Action: Pub/Sub event bus as single integration layer[cite: 4]. AS decouples all producers from all consumers[cite: 4].
Org Readiness
3 / 5
Domain SMEs exist — planners, quality managers, procurement directors[cite: 4]. No ML/AI team in-house[cite: 4]. HITL-ready: named approvers identifiable for all five high-risk models across all domains[cite: 4].
Action: HITL visibility is the organisational unlock[cite: 4]. Deploy HITL interface first, expand model coverage second[cite: 4].

Scoring methodology: Aligned to the Google Cloud AI Adoption Framework (five capability dimensions) and the Gartner AI Readiness Assessment model[cite: 4]. Scale 1–5, where 1 = no capability and 5 = fully mature[cite: 4]. Scores derived from structured discovery sessions against each dimension's capability indicators with ClaraVis stakeholders S-01 through S-09[cite: 4]. Sources: Google Cloud AI Adoption Framework (cloud.google.com/adoption-framework) · Gartner AI Readiness Model (2024)[cite: 4].

Eight use cases mapped to modules, pain points, and EU AI Act risk classification[cite: 4]. Five are classified High-Risk under Annex III — classification mapped to specific Annex III provisions (noted per card)[cite: 4]. All High-Risk models require SHAP explanations per inference, documented HITL checkpoints, versioned Model Cards, and a DPIA under GDPR Art. 35 before go-live[cite: 4]. Three are not high-risk and operate without mandatory HITL, but all are auditable[cite: 4]. Model references specify capability requirements — specific model versions are tracked in Vertex AI Model Registry and subject to change[cite: 4].

Section 06

Business Requirements

Every requirement below is traceable to a stakeholder concern, a regulatory obligation, or a business pain point identified in the preceding sections[cite: 4]. Prioritised using MoSCoW — BR-01 through BR-09 are Must Have; BR-10 and BR-11 are Should Have as they refine delivery of Must Have capabilities[cite: 4]. Each requirement includes a measurable acceptance criterion[cite: 4]. Eleven requirements covering the primary commercial outcomes, compliance obligations, and governance boundaries[cite: 4].

BR-01

Demand forecast error reduced from 34% to ≤12% within 12 months

Forecast MAPE on imaging system components must be reduced from 34% to ≤12% within 12 months of DemandIQ go-live[cite: 4]. Measured at SKU level across all 6 manufacturing sites using SAP IBP actuals as the baseline (FY2024 12-month rolling MAPE, n=847 SKUs)[cite: 4]. Improvement must be attributable to the ML model, not safety stock increases[cite: 4]. Acceptance criterion: ≤12% MAPE sustained for 3 consecutive calendar months as measured in the Vertex AI monitoring dashboard, validated against SAP IBP actuals by the Supply Chain Analytics team[cite: 4].

Must Have DemandIQ
InventoryOrchestrator
BR-02

Supplier risk events surfaced ≥30 days before operational impact

SupplierSentinel must detect and surface material supplier risk events — financial distress, geopolitical exposure, quality deterioration — with a minimum 30-day lead time before operational impact[cite: 4]. Current detection lag is 11 days (FY2024 post-incident review across 12 disruption events)[cite: 4]. Lead time measured as the interval between model alert timestamp and confirmed impact event date[cite: 4]. Acceptance criterion: ≥30-day lead time across ≥80% of material risk events in the 6-month post-go-live monitoring window, reviewed in joint quarterly business review between SupplierSentinel product owner and CPO[cite: 4].

Must Have SupplierSentinel
BR-03

NCR resolution time reduced from 23 days to ≤5 days

Average NCR resolution time must fall from 23 days to ≤5 days within 6 months of QualityTrace go-live[cite: 4]. Baseline: FY2024 average across 143 NCR events, measured from event creation in Veeva Vault to Quality Director sign-off[cite: 4]. Reduction achieved through automated device genealogy tracing, intelligent triage, and structured HITL workflow — not by lowering NCR quality standards or reclassifying resolution events[cite: 4]. Acceptance criterion: ≤5-day average NCR resolution time over any rolling 30-day window post go-live, validated from Veeva Vault timestamps by Quality Director and independently auditable under ISO 13485 internal audit[cite: 4].

Must Have QualityTrace
BR-04

MDR Article 87 vigilance reporting within 72-hour SLA — architecturally satisfied

MDR Article 87 requires notification of serious incidents within 72 hours of becoming aware[cite: 4]. QualityTrace must satisfy this SLA architecturally — by producing a complete, structured vigilance report draft within 72 hours of event ingestion from Veeva Vault — not by accelerating existing manual processes[cite: 4]. HITL Quality Director approval is the final gate before submission[cite: 4].

Must Have QualityTrace
MDR Art. 87
BR-05

CSRD Scope 3 reporting automated, real-time, and compliant from FY2025

CSRD Scope 3 Category 1 (purchased goods) and Category 4 (upstream transportation) must be reported in real-time from FY2025 mandatory reporting date[cite: 4]. ScopeTracer must ingest data from all six freight portals and 310 Ariba supplier nodes without manual intervention[cite: 4]. The output must be CSRD-compliant and auditable — not a dashboard estimate[cite: 4].

Must Have ScopeTracer
